How to Write a Resume That Gets Noticed: A Complete Guide for Job Seekers

Career Tips · April 7, 2026 · 9 min

Submitted 30 resumes, but received very few responses? Have you ever thought that the problem may not be your ability, but how to write your resume?

According to some HR reports, they spend an average of 6 seconds scanning a resume. This means that you have to catch their attention instantly to have a chance to advance to the interview stage. But what kind of resume can be seen? This article shares the pitfalls I have experienced and the techniques I have learned from the perspective of job seekers.

Basic structure of resume: not more is better, but clearer is better

Let me start with a misunderstanding: a resume is not a complete record of your life. Many people will write down every job and project since college, and the result is that their resume becomes a 3-4 page long essay. What is the effect of this? HR will skip faster.

A good resume should contain these parts:

Personal information: Name, contact number, Email, LinkedIn or personal website. This is the only way HR will contact you, so make sure this information is correct. I’ve heard of people filling in their phone numbers incorrectly and missing out on great opportunities.

Work experience: Arrange in reverse chronological order, with the most recent job first. Just write 3-5 key points for each job, no more. This is the core of the entire resume. I will explain how to write it in detail later.

Education: List highest education and related degrees. If you have been working for more than 5 years, the educational field can be simplified and there is no need to list high school.

Skills: List job-related skills, such as Excel, Adobe Suite, specific software, or language proficiency. Don’t fill in function words like “interpersonal communication” and change it to specific tools and techniques.

Self-introduction or career summary: Optional, but if written well, can be the face of the entire resume.

The structure is like this, the focus is simple and clear. A good resume should be one page, at most one and a half pages. Don’t expect HR to read your life story carefully.

Quantitative results: “Improving performance” is not as good as “increasing 30% within half a year”

This is the most common mistake. When many people write resumes, they will use descriptions like this: “Responsible for marketing and promotion, with remarkable results”, “Improved Facebook fan interaction”, “Improved customer service process”. It all sounds good, but the problem is: without specific numbers, there is no convincing.

When HR scans resumes, their eyes will automatically track the numbers. Because numbers are the most objective evidence. Try changing it to this:

“Through A/B testing and remarketing activities, the website conversion rate increased from 2.1% to 2.8% within half a year, adding 150,000 yuan in revenue to the company.” “Implementing Instagram post strategy adjustments, the fan interaction rate increased by 45% within 3 months, and a single post reached a maximum of 8,000 people.” “Optimized customer service SOP, shortening the average response time from 4 hours to 2.5 hours, and improving customer satisfaction to 4.6/5.”

See the difference? Numbers make everything concrete and convincing. How to quantify the results? Ask yourself: How much revenue did this project increase or how much cost did it save? How many participants or data were touched? By what percentage did time efficiency improve? Not every result can be quantified, but 80% is fine. Taking the time to organize this data will greatly improve the competitiveness of your resume.

Via ATS system: Keywords are more important than literary talent

In 2026, most medium and large companies (especially technology, finance, and foreign companies) use ATS (Applicant Tracking System) to automatically screen resumes. Before HR can actually see your resume, you must first pass the ATS level. The working logic of ATS is to search for keywords in job descriptions. Only resumes that hit the keywords will be displayed to HR.

Practical practice: Before applying for a job vacancy, paste the job description in Word and circle 5-10 keywords (skills, tools, professional titles, industry words), and then check whether these words appear naturally in your resume. For example, if the job vacancy says “Familiar with GA4, SQL, A/B testing”, it is not enough to use “website analysis” in your resume. You must clearly write the original words of “GA4”, “SQL” and “A/B testing”.

⚠️ Don’t stuff keywords at the end of the page or hide them in white fonts (modern ATS will detect cheating), and don’t stuff irrelevant keywords - if you really get into the interview, HR will break the news as soon as you ask.

Differences in resume emphasis for different positions

Marketing, administration, business, design - different positions, HR looks at resumes from different angles.

Marketing Position: HR will look at your campaign effectiveness, data analysis capabilities, and creative achievements. If you have done social marketing, you should list the fan growth rate and interaction rate; if you have done content marketing, you should explain the frequency of posting and traffic growth. Data is king.

Administrative Positions: Being organized, careful, and able to execute are key. Explain how you optimize processes, reduce error rates, and improve department collaboration. HR will look at your project management skills and cross-department communication record.

Business Position: Performance speaks for itself. List your sales, customer acquisition numbers, and customer retention rates. If there are large customers, you can list the customer size and cooperation period. Also mention your negotiating skills - how did you win this client?

Design Position: Portfolio is more important than words. But your resume still needs to explain what design tools you have used, what types of projects you have participated in (graphic design, UI/UX, visual marketing, etc.), and the commercial value brought by the design results. For example: “Redesign the product interface and reduce the churn rate of new users from 35% to 22%.”

General principle: Emphasize the most important indicators for this position.

Avoid common landmines: add points to your resume, not take away points

Once you have written good content, don’t let it get ruined by these small details.

Long text, too much nonsense: “During this position, I proactively learned and applied various marketing tools, and worked with the team to achieve the company’s marketing goals.” This sentence can be changed to: “Using Google Analytics and HubSpot, in conjunction with SEO strategies, increased website traffic by 65%.” Cut off the empty words such as “active” and “cooperate together” and go straight to the point.

Confusing typesetting, different font sizes: Use a consistent font (Arial or Microsoft Black), a consistent size (text 11-12pt), and a unified format. Don’t use 7 colors, 3 fonts – it just looks unprofessional.

Titles are vague or oversimplified: Don’t write “employee” or “staff.” Write “Marketing Planning”, “Community Manager” and “Business Development Specialist”. Job titles help HR quickly identify your role.

Never Update: Review your resume at least every six months. Make sure your latest work experience is at the top, and expired skills can be removed. An outdated resume will make HR doubt whether you are seriously looking for a job.

How to write an autobiography: Don’t start with “My name is XXX”

An autobiography is often the weakest link in a resume. Many people wrote: “My name is Wang Xiaoming. I am 28 years old and was born in Taipei. I am a diligent, serious and responsible person. I have loved marketing since I was a child…” This is a running account, not an autobiography. HR already knows your name and what you have done from your resume. What he wants to see is: Why you?

The golden formula of an autobiography: First, give a brief background (1 sentence): “I am a marketing planner with 5 years of marketing experience.” Second, explain the career transition or core driving force (2-3 sentences): “After the first campaign in my previous company successfully increased sales by 300,000, I found that I am particularly passionate about data-driven marketing.” Third, demonstrate core capabilities (2-3 sentences): “I am good at using GA to analyze user behavior and plan A/B Test and quickly adjust strategies based on data. In the past year, the number of followers of the social channel I am responsible for has increased by 120%.” Fourth, explain why you want to join this company (1-2 sentences). Such an autobiography should be no longer than 150 words but clearly articulate your value proposition.

FAQ

Q: Should I write down all my work experience?

A: No need. If you have worked for more than 10 years, you only need to write down your work for the last 10 years. Earlier work can be omitted unless particularly relevant. Brevity is better than completeness.

Q: What format should I use to submit my resume?

A: Unless specifically requested by the company, please submit in PDF format. The layout of the PDF will not change depending on the computer environment of the other party, ensuring that the layout you put so much effort into is seen correctly. However, please note that when the ATS system parses the PDF, it may misread the contents of tables and columns, so the layout is mainly single-column text to avoid complex tables.

Q: Is it necessary to write an autobiography?

A: If the company does not specifically require an autobiography, the self-introduction block on the resume will suffice. But if there is an autobiography field, remember to take the time to write with quality. This is an opportunity to show your personal style.

There is no magic in writing a resume. The most effective way is simple: Speak out your results in numbers, use ATS keywords, format them clearly, create a page, and then adjust the focus according to the position before submitting. Your next job is out there, just show HR that you’re worthy of an interview within 6 seconds.
