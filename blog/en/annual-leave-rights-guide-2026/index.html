2026 Annual Leave Guide for Taiwan: Eligibility, Calculation, Forfeiture Rules, and Salary Compensation

Labor Rights · April 12, 2026 · 10 min

Do you really understand how many days you have special leave?

Special leave (special leave) is a paid leave guaranteed by the Labor Standards Act, and it is also one of the benefits that office workers care most about. But many people are unclear about how many days of special leave they have. They don’t even know that if the special leave is not used up, the company must convert wages to you according to law. At the beginning of Q2, it is a good time to take stock of this year’s special leave quota and plan vacations. This article explains clearly the provisions of the Labor Standards Law on special leave at once, so that you can know where your rights are.

How to calculate the number of special leave days? Complete comparison table

According to Article 38 of the Labor Standards Act, the number of days of special leave is calculated based on your length of service under the “same employer”:

  • 6 months and less than 1 year: 3 days
  • 1 year but less than 2 years: 7 days
  • 2 years but less than 3 years: 10 days
  • From 3 years to 5 years: 14 days
  • From 5 years to 10 years: 15 days
  • Over 10 years: add 1 day for each additional year, up to a maximum of 30 days

For example: If you join the job on July 1, 2024, and your seniority has reached 1 year but less than 2 years by April 2026, your special leave for this period should be 7 days. If you are not sure how many months you have been employed, you can use a date calculator to calculate the exact number of days you have been employed.

What is the difference between the “annual system” and the “calendar system” of special leave?

There are two systems for granting special leave, which are very different:

Anniversary system is based on your arrival date, and you will get new special leave days every time you reach a certain number of years. For example, if you start work on March 15th, your special leave period is from March 15th of each year to March 14th of the following year. This is the original design of the Labor Standards Act, which is most beneficial to workers.

Calendar year system means that the company unifies the period of special leave from January 1st to December 31st, and converts the number of days according to your seniority ratio in that year. Many companies will adopt the calendar year system for administrative convenience, but the premise is that the rights and interests of workers cannot be harmed.

No matter which system the company uses, there is an iron rule: the converted number of special leave days cannot be less than the result of the annual system. If a company calculates fewer days using the calendar year system than the annual system, it is illegal. It is recommended that you count both at once to confirm whether the company has paid you less.

Special leave scheduling rights: Who has the final say?

Many people are confused about this and think that special leave requires the approval of the supervisor. Article 38, Paragraph 2 of the Labor Standards Law clearly stipulates that the arrangement of special leave is, in principle, decided by the “worker” himself. In other words, you basically have the final say on which day you want to take special leave.

However, the law also has a proviso: if the employer has urgent business needs, it can negotiate with the workers to make adjustments. But “negotiation” is not “rejection” - the company cannot unilaterally deny you a special leave, and can only discuss changing the time with you. If you encounter your boss who says, “There are not enough people that day, so you are not allowed to take special leave.” He must provide specific reasons for the operation, and negotiate an alternative date that you agree with.

Common violations include: the company stipulates that special leave must be applied for one month in advance, supervisors have the right to veto applications for special leave, and it is completely prohibited to apply for special leave during peak seasons. If these provisions substantially deprive you of your special leave rights, it may violate the Labor Standards Act.

What should I do if my special vacation is not over? The company has to pay you

This may be the most important provision: at the end of the year or when the contract is terminated, if you still have unused special leave, the employer “should” convert it into wages and pay it to you. Note that the law uses “should”, not “should” - which means that this is not optional and the company must provide it.

The calculation method for unvacated wages: Divide your “regular working hour wages” for the last month by 30, and then multiply by the number of unvacated days. Assume that your monthly salary is 40,000 yuan, and you have 3 days of special leave that you have not taken. Your untaken salary is 40,000 ÷ 30 × 3 = 4,000 yuan.

There are several common illegal practices that you should pay special attention to:

  • The company said “the special leave will be void if it is not completed” - it is illegal. Unless you don’t want to take the leave yourself and agree to defer it to the next year after negotiation, your salary will be converted into salary.
  • The company said “those who leave the company will not have unpaid wages” - it is illegal. Regardless of whether you resigned on your own or were laid off, the unused portion of the special leave must be calculated.
  • The company mixes special leave with sick leave and personal leave - it is illegal. Special leave is an independent type of leave and cannot be mixed.

5 common workplace traps when taking special leave

In practice, the following situations often occur in Taiwan’s workplace, and you should know how to deal with them:

**Trap 1: There is no special leave after less than one year on the job. **Since the Labor Standards Law was revised in 2017, employees will be entitled to 3 days of special leave after 6 months of age. If the company is still using the old rule of “only taking a special leave after one year of service”, it is no longer legal.

**Trap 2: Scheduling special holidays requires supervisor approval. **As for the administrative process, it is okay for you to fill in a form to notify the company, but your supervisor cannot deny your special leave by saying “disapproval”.

**Trap 3: At the end of the year, you are suddenly asked to use up your special holiday. **Some companies require employees to take compulsory leave at the end of the year in order to avoid paying unvacated wages. It would also be controversial if the arrangement was forced rather than negotiated.

**Trap 4: Special leave cannot be used during the notice of resignation. **The law does not provide this. You can still use special leave during the preview period.

**Trap 5: The probation period does not count towards seniority. **The Labor Standards Law does not have the concept of “probationary period”. Your seniority starts from the first day you join the job.

What should I do if my rights and interests are infringed?

If you find that your company has a problem with its special leave practices, there are several ways to deal with it:

The first step is to report it to the company’s human resources department. Sometimes the human resources system is set incorrectly or the supervisor does not understand the regulations, which can be solved through internal communication. It is recommended to communicate in writing (email or text messages via messaging software) and leave records.

In the second step, if the company does not handle the matter, it can submit an application for labor dispute mediation to the labor bureau of each county or city. Mediation is free, and your employer cannot take any adverse action against you during the mediation.

The third step is to report directly to the Labor Bureau. Employers who violate regulations related to special leave may be fined NT$20,000 to NT$1 million.

No matter which route you take, the most important thing is to preserve the evidence. Your attendance records, special leave application records, and the unpaid wages field on your payslip are all key supporting documents. It is recommended that you regularly organize these materials into PDF backups for emergencies.

FAQ

Can I take special leave in “hours”?

The Labor Standards Act stipulates that the minimum unit of special leave is “day”, but if both parties agree, the leave can be taken in half-day or hourly units. In other words, if the company agrees, you can only take a half-day special leave. However, the company cannot force you to take time off in hourly units, thereby increasing the threshold for you to take time off.

Are contract workers or dispatch workers also entitled to special leave?

As long as you have a labor contract relationship with your employer, whether you are a full-time employee, a contract employee, or even part-time employee (hourly wage system), you have the right to take special leave. The difference is that the number of special days off for part-time work will be calculated on a pro-rata basis. The dispatch company is responsible for special leave for dispatched workers, and their seniority is also based on their seniority with the dispatch company.

Can the special leave be deferred to next year?

Yes, but only if both parties agree. Deferral is limited to one time, that is, it can be deferred to the next year at most. If the leave is still not taken after deferral, the employer must convert the untaken wages to you.

If I change jobs, can my seniority from the previous company be brought to the new company?

Generally speaking no. The seniority for special leave is based on the “same employer” and will be recalculated if the company changes. However, if a public institution is reorganized or transferred and you are retained by the new company, your original seniority should continue to be recognized.

The information in this article is based on the Labor Standards Act and relevant interpretations from the Ministry of Labor, and is updated as of April 2026.

Special leave is part of your job remuneration, not a favor from the company. Figure out how many days off you have, how to schedule them, and what to do if you don’t take all the time off. These are the basic skills to protect yourself. Take advantage of this month to take stock of this year’s special leave quota, and take the leave you need with peace of mind.
