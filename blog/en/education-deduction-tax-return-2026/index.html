2026 Tax Filing: Education, Tuition, and Tutoring Deductions Plus New Brackets to Save NT$20,000

Tax & Finance · March 29, 2026 · 9 min

When filing taxes in May, you may have wasted your deductions on these education expenses.

Many office workers spend tens of thousands of dollars every year on their children’s education (tuition fees, tutoring, talent classes, etc.), but they don’t know how much can be deducted when filing taxes. The government will not proactively remind you of some deductions at all, and you can only reduce taxes by actively claiming them. Many new systems are on the way for the tax filing season of 2026 (reporting 114 years of income). If you have children’s education expenses, you must read this article carefully.

Education-related deductible items

1. Special deduction for children’s education tuition (most common)

As long as you have children studying in “tertiary institutions recognized by the Republic of China”, each person can deduct 25,000 education tuition fees per year (need to be paid in the tax year or paid in installments).

  • Conditions: The children are studying in colleges and universities recognized by the Ministry of Education at home and abroad (university, technical vocational, and fourth-level skills).
  • Amount: Maximum limit of $25,000 per child per year
  • Multiple children: 3 are studying in college → each can deduct NT$25,000, totaling NT$75,000
  • Not applicable: Air University, Air College, the first 3 years of the fifth college (from the first to the third college), language school
  • Common loopholes: Dormitory fees and book fees are usually not deductible, only “tuition fees + miscellaneous fees” can be deducted; confirm whether the payment receipt says “tuition fees” or “accommodation fees”

2. Special deduction for preschool education (significantly increased in 2026)

The new tax system for 114 years has been significantly increased, and the wealth exclusion clause has been canceled (previously, comprehensive income tax rates above 20% were not applicable, but now all tax rates are applicable):

  • 1st child: 150,000 yuan/year (original 120,000 yuan → increased)
  • Second and above: 225,000 yuan/year (original 225,000 yuan, maintained)
  • Conditions: Child’s age under 6 years old (originally under 5 years old)
  • Applicable: enrolled in registered kindergartens, childcare centers, or nanny fees

⚠️ Common misunderstanding: Many old articles state that “12,000 yuan + children under 5 years old” is the old system for 2024, and is no longer applicable to 2026 tax returns. If you see a guideline that says $12,000 in 2026, skip it.

3. Children’s education expenses (private high school and below)

Tuition fees for private junior high schools and private high schools (excluding higher vocational schools) can be deducted more when calculating the deduction for dependent relatives. However, the method is not “direct deduction”. Instead, a special note is made when reporting dependent relatives, and the tax authorities calculate the tax reduction effect.

  • No deduction for public schools: There is no additional deduction for government-subsidized public high schools.
  • Education fee subsidy for children of civil servants: If you are a civil servant, this amount can be directly subsidized by the government, and there is no need to deduct it from your tax return.

4. Tutoring classes and talent classes? Usually no deductions will be made, but there are exceptions

General cram schools and music, art, and sports talent courses are usually not deductible when filing comprehensive income taxes. There are two exceptions:

  • Long-term care “home care course fee”: If you participate in a government-approved care training course (the long-term care special discount will be increased to 180,000 in 2026), the fee can be deducted
  • Lifelong Learning Courses (subsidy available in some areas): Some counties and cities provide “elderly learning” or “senior education” subsidies, but they are not tax deduction items, but direct subsidies.

How to ensure you don’t miss these deductions?

1. Prepare complete proof of payment

  • University: tuition bill, payment receipt (must be attached to the tax return when sending for inspection)
  • Kindergarten/Nanny: Monthly fee payment certificate or annual invoice (the original or photocopy must be kept for future reference when filing taxes in May)
  • Long-term care course: Course receipt + Sponsor’s registration certificate

2. Be sure to check the box when filing online

In the Ministry of Finance’s electronic declaration system, go to “Basic Information” → “Dependent Relatives” and check whether the fields for each child are missing:

  • Check “Education Fee” + Amount (University)
  • “Preschool” check + amount (under 6 years old)
  • Check “Long-term care” (if there is a person who needs care)

3. Choose one of the multi-factor authentication methods

2026 tax filing 6 login methods: mobile phone authentication / health insurance card + registration password / natural person certificate / mobile natural person certificate / account number + query code / financial certificate. Mobile phone authentication is recommended, login in as fast as 1 minute.

4. Go to the tax collection office in person to confirm

If you are not confident about the online system, you can go to the tax collection office in person to declare at the counter with all your receipts and ID cards. The staff will help you check to see if you have missed any deductions.

5. Pay attention to time limits

Certain deductions have a requirement that “the tax has been paid during the year” (for example, tuition fees must be paid from January to December 2025 in order to be deducted when filing a tax return in May 2026). The applicable years for prepayment or deferred payment will be different.

FAQ

Q1: How is “225,000 after the second place” calculated for preschool children? A: Each of the 2nd + 3rd +… children (under 6 years old) in the family can deduct 225,000. 1st place 150,000 + 2nd place 225,000 + 3rd place 225,000 = 600,000. The tax savings are significant for a family with 3 young children.

Q2: If my child is a freshman, can the tuition fee for enrollment in September 2025 be deducted from the tax return in May 2026? A:Yes. Tuition fees paid in the second half of 2025 are deductible items for “2025 year” and should be declared in May 2026 when reporting annual income. However, the tuition for the next semester that starts in February 2026 belongs to the “2026 year” and cannot be deducted until May 2027.

Q3: How to determine the order of smart deductions? A: Standard deduction vs. itemized deductions. It is more cost-effective for most families to directly use the standard deduction (262,000 for a couple), unless you have large medical expenses, donations, and interest on home purchases that can be listed in excess of 262,000. However, special deductions (education, infant care, long-term care) can be deducted regardless of standard or enumerated deductions.

Q4: For single, childless people, what should you pay attention to when filing taxes in 2026? A: ① The standard deduction is increased to 131,000, ② The salary deduction is increased to 227,000, ③ Basic living expenses are 213,000 – the total tax exemption threshold for the three items is increased by approximately 23,000. Basically, there is no tax on those with a monthly salary of less than 40,000.

Filing taxes in May is not only an “obligation” but also an annual opportunity to legally reduce taxes. Especially families with children’s education expenses + elders who need care should carefully check all available deductions. Before the tax filing deadline (May 31), have all supporting documents ready to make sure every penny is accounted for.
