2026 Currency Exchange Guide: 6 Channels, Fees, Airport & ATM Reality in Taiwan

Personal Finance · February 1, 2026 · 9 min

When traveling abroad, studying overseas, or shopping online across borders, currency exchange is an unavoidable task. However, for the same exchange demand of US$1,000, if operated through different channels and at different times, the price difference may be NT$800 to NT$2,000**. The latest version of this article in 2026 takes stock of the actual costs of 6 types of currency exchange channels, from exchange rate types and fee structures to the calculation logic and DCC traps of 1.5% for overseas card swiping, helping you choose the right method for every currency exchange.

1. Understand exchange rate quotes: spot, cash, buy, sell

Open any bank’s advertised exchange rate page and you will see four columns of numbers. Let us first distinguish these four words clearly, and all subsequent comparisons will be based on this basis.

Spot Rate (Spot Rate): used for transfers between foreign currency accounts, online foreign exchange settlement, and overseas credit card payments. No physical banknotes are delivered, banking operations are cheaper and the exchange rate is most favorable to customers.

Cash Rate (Cash Rate): used for cash buying and selling. Banks have to import, keep and count cash, which requires high operating costs and the exchange rate is worse than the spot.

Buying Rate (Buying Rate): The price at which the bank buys foreign currency from you - look at this column when you exchange foreign currency for Taiwan dollars.

Selling Rate (Selling Rate): The price at which the bank sells foreign currency to you - look at this column when you exchange Taiwan dollars for foreign currency.

In practice** spot rates are 0.5%–1.5%** better than cash rates** (depending on the currency). Taking the U.S. dollar as an example, during the observation period in the first half of 2026, the price difference between spot selling and cash selling was approximately NT$0.30/USD, and the difference in exchange for 1,000 US dollars was approximately NT$300. The price difference of Japanese yen is even more obvious, with a common price of NT$0.04/yen, and the difference is about NT$400 when exchanging 100,000 yen.

**Conclusion: If you can use immediate payment, you don’t need cash. ** However, you need to use banknotes at the tourist site, and eventually you have to exchange them for cash - at this time, you need to rely on the “choosing the right currency exchange channel” mentioned below to keep the cost down.

① Online foreign exchange settlement (the most cost-effective legal channel)

Both Mega Bank and Bank of Taiwan provide 24-hour online foreign exchange settlement: log in to online banking and fill in the form → agree on the bank withdrawal branch → collect it at the branch (or pick it up at the airport) within 7 working days. Spot exchange rate + 0 handling fee + no need to queue, suitable for people who plan more than 2 weeks before going abroad. Major currencies such as the Japanese yen, US dollar, euro, and renminbi are all supported.

② Bank App Spot Currency Exchange

Many banks (Taishin, Yushan, Cathay Pacific, Wing Fung…) have launched 24-hour currency exchange in the app, which allows you to directly exchange from your Taiwan dollar account to the foreign currency account of your peers, with spot exchange rates and no handling fees**. The foreign currency exchanged can be left in the account and can be spent online, and you can go to the counter to collect cash before leaving the country.

③ Bank counter

Through traditional channels, the exchange rate of “cash sales” is unfavorable, and some banks charge a handling fee of less than 100 yuan/transaction. The advantage is that large amounts (over RMB 500,000) require double verification in person for greater peace of mind, and travel expenses or overseas remittances can be processed at the same time.

④ Foreign currency ATM (in Taiwan)

Direct debit from Taiwan dollar account to exchange foreign currency cash. The exchange rate is based on the banknotes of each bank (some banks have discounts), and the inter-bank handling fee is only NT$5. For example: When Yushan Bank withdraws money from a Taiwan dollar account, the exchange rate is cash, plus 5 cents for the U.S. dollar and 0.1 cents for the Japanese yen.

The calculation method for withdrawals from foreign currency account is different: Mega has a fixed 100 yuan per transaction, Taishin has changed to “the difference between the spot exchange rate and the cash exchange rate × 0.5” with a minimum of 100 yuan since 2025-06-06, and Yushan has a “foreign currency amount × exchange difference × 0.9” with a minimum of 100 yuan.

⑤ Airport bank counters and foreign currency cash exchange machines

Both Taoyuan Airport Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 have Taiwan Bank and Mega Bank counters open 24 hours a day. 0 handling fee for converting New Taiwan dollars to foreign currencies (policies of both companies), but the exchange rate of “cash sales” is slightly worse than that of urban banks (affected by 24h labor costs and the urgency of tourists).

Peach Machine will add a “foreign currency banknote exchange machine” (foreign currency banknote exchange machine) starting from 2024. There is no handling fee for banknote exchange. The exchange rate is based on the bank notice. It is a good emergency solution if you forget to change money before departure. But please note: the machine only accepts banknotes of specific denominations, and the change method varies from machine to machine.

⑥ Overseas local ATM withdrawal

Cash cards with the PLUS or Cirrus logo can be used to withdraw local currency at overseas ATMs. The exchange rate is close to the spot (converted by international organizations), but each transaction requires Taiwanese bank overseas withdrawal fee of 75-150 yuan + local ATM handling fee (Japanese 7-11 ATM about 220 yen / American bank about USD 3-5). It is cost-effective to withdraw large amounts (over NT$5,000), but it is least cost-effective to withdraw multiple small amounts.

3. Trial calculation of the cost of swiping cards overseas vs. exchanging cash

Many people are confused about whether they should swipe their cards or exchange cash when abroad. Let’s first look at the calculation formula for credit card swiping:

Total cost of overseas card swiping = foreign exchange exchange by international organizations (near the spot exchange rate) + overseas handling fee 1.5%

Banks in Taiwan of the three major international organizations, VISA, Mastercard and JCB, charge a uniform 1.5% (international organization 1% + 0.5% bank surcharge limit approved by the Financial Supervisory Commission), and American Express 2%. This 1.5% is included in the bill amount and is not an additional deduction.

Trial calculation: Buy 100,000 yen goods in Japan

  • Method A: First exchange 100,000 yen in cash online in Taiwan: Based on the spot exchange rate + 0 handling fee - assuming a conversion of 0.218, the cost is about NT$21,800
  • Method B: Overseas card swiping: Swipe the card at the spot exchange rate (also around 0.218) + 1.5% handling fee - the cost is about NT$22,127 (difference NT$327)
  • Method C: Airport Cash Counter: Sell in cash 0.221 + 0 handling fee - Cost approximately NT$22,100
  • Method D: Withdraw 100,000 yen from an overseas ATM: Spot 0.218 + Taiwan overseas withdrawal fee 100 yuan + Japanese ATM handling fee 220 yen (approximately NT$48) - cost approximately NT$21,948

Conclusion: Online foreign exchange settlement (Method A) is the cheapest, but when the cash exchanged on site is used up, Swiping a card overseas (Method B) is more cost-effective than exchanging cash at the airport (Method C). ATM withdrawal (method D) is reasonable as an emergency solution to replenish money during the trip, but it is not suitable for withdrawing the full amount at the beginning.

DCC Trap: The store asks you “Would you like to settle in Taiwan dollars or local currency?”

DCC (Dynamic Currency Conversion) is a service where foreign stores or ATMs actively ask whether to “display the amount in Taiwan dollars”. Please always choose local currency: DCC uses the store’s custom exchange rate, which is usually 3%-7% worse than the exchange rate of international organizations. Plus your overseas handling fee of 1.5% will still be charged, which is equivalent to a double markup.

The hidden definition of overseas consumption

“Overseas consumption” is not just about physically swiping your card abroad. Netflix, Spotify, Disney+, Apple One, Uber Eats, Foodpanda, ChatGPT Plus subscription, etc. are all overseas consumption, and each transaction will be charged a 1.5% handling fee. It is recommended to concentrate this type of subscription on a card with good overseas rewards (SinoPac, Taishin Richart, Far East Roca+, HSBC Cashback, etc.), and offset the 1.5% or even earn back.

4. Judgment of currency exchange timing

Large-amount currency exchange: batching is common sense

If you want to exchange foreign currency of more than NT$500,000, enter the market in 3-5 batches within 1-2 months to avoid the risk of single-point price fluctuations. The common currency amplitude during the year is 5%–10%, and it can be pulled close to the average in batches.

Look at the central bank’s announcement and interest rate spread trends

The Taiwan dollar against the U.S. dollar is mainly affected by the U.S. dollar index (DXY), Taiwan-U.S. interest rate differentials, and exporters’ U.S. dollar settlement needs. The Fed’s interest rate cut/raise meeting week is highly volatile, so you can choose to observe the direction 2-3 days after the meeting before entering the market.

5. Currency exchange habits for popular tourist currencies

Japanese Yen (JPY): Fluctuates greatly, and online foreign exchange settlement is relatively stable in batches 2 weeks before departure. Most large stores and convenience stores in Japan accept credit cards, but small country stores, shrines, and vendors still often require cash**.

United States Dollar (USD): Some countries in Southeast Asia and Central and South America also accept US dollar cash. It is recommended to bring small denominations (USD 1, 5, 10) for easy change.

Hong Kong Dollar (HKD): In most places in Hong Kong, credit cards and electronic payments are the main payment methods. You only need to carry 1,000–2,000 HKD.

Korean Won (KRW): Card swiping is extremely popular in South Korea (night market vendors all accept T-money), and only 50,000-100,000 Korean won can be exchanged for tips and emergencies.

Euro (EUR) and British Pound (GBP): Easy to use, but cash is mainly in 5/10/20 denominations, avoid 100/200 banknotes (many stores do not accept them).

Renminbi (CNY): In China, WeChat and Alipay are the main payment methods. Cash is almost non-circulating, and a small amount of cash is sufficient for backup.

6. FAQ

**Q1: ​​Why is the spot exchange rate better than the cash exchange rate? ** Spot is a digital transfer between accounts. There is no physical banknote delivery, and the bank does not need to bear the costs of storage, transportation, counting, etc. The quoted price of cash cards includes cash handling costs, so it is 0.5%–1.5% worse than spot.

**Q2: Are the banknotes for online exchange settlement new or old? ** According to each bank’s policy, most banks do not guarantee new or old banknotes, but will issue banknotes that are “fit for circulation” (without damage or stains). If you want to give a gift or exchange new banknotes, it is recommended to go to a branch to make a reservation at the counter.

**Q3: Are the foreign currency cash exchange machines at the airport reliable? ** The Peach Machine foreign currency cash exchange machine is set up by the bank. The exchange rate is reported according to the bank card. There is no handling fee for currency exchange. It is a good choice for 24-hour emergency relief. However, the machine only accepts specific denominations and may not be able to provide change. Please read the machine instructions before using it.

**Q4: Is it worth getting an overseas rewards credit card? ** If your monthly overseas card usage + overseas subscription total exceeds NT$5,000, you can completely offset the 1.5% handling fee by applying for an overseas card with a rebate of ≥ 3%, or even make money back. If the amount is less than this amount, the management cost will be high.

**Q5: If DCC has selected the local currency, will it still be charged a handling fee? ** 1.5% will still be charged because the handling fee is generated by the action of “cross-border credit card swiping” (1% for international organizations + 0.5% for banks). But you avoid the extra 3%–7% water exchange rate of DCC, and you still save money overall.
