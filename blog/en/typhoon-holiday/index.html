Typhoon Days Off in Taiwan: 2026 Updated Suspension Standards & Worker Rights

Life Tips · January 20, 2026 · 10 min

Whenever a typhoon strikes, the question most people ask is “Will classes be suspended tomorrow?” Taiwan will encounter more than one class suspension almost every year during the typhoon season. However, from decision-making timing, wind and rain compliance standards to salary processing, many details are actually unclear to most people. This article starts from the source of the law and clarifies all the practical rights and interests you need to know at once.

Picture: The city scene when a typhoon hits (picture placeholder, to be added)

Legal basis: “Measures for suspending work and class work due to natural disasters”

Taiwan’s typhoon holiday is not something that can be issued with just one sentence of administrative order, but has a clear legal source. The core basis is the “Regulations on Suspension of Work and Class Work in Natural Disasters” formulated by the Office of Personnel and Administration of the Executive Yuan in 1996 and subsequently revised many times. This method standardizes:

  • Which natural disasters will trigger the suspension of classes (typhoons, heavy rains, earthquakes, floods, etc.)
  • The authority and responsibility for the suspension of classes (central vs. local)
  • Objective standards for wind and rain compliance
  • Announcement timing and media communication method
  • Labor attendance and payroll processing principles

In other words, when you see county and city governments announcing the suspension of classes, there is a whole set of regulations and processes in operation, not the personal preference of the chief executive. Only by understanding this mechanism can you control your rights and interests during typhoons.

Who decides? Decision-making rights and responsibilities are clarified at once

Many people think that the suspension of classes is a unified announcement from the central government, but it is not. Decision-making power lies in the hands of local chiefs:

  • Agencies and schools within municipal districts: shall be announced by the mayor
  • Institutions and schools within the jurisdiction of **county (city): decided by the county (city) mayor
  • Governments at all levels can also authorize decisions to lower-level heads based on topography, landforms, and historical experience in disaster prevention (for example, mountainous towns and villages can make independent announcements)

This is why there is a phenomenon of “neighboring counties and cities let one side go and the other side not” - because the decision-making power is decentralized, and each county and city government makes independent judgments by referring to the meteorological data, geographical characteristics, and past disasters in its own jurisdiction. It rains equally in Taipei City and New Taipei City, but different decisions may be made due to different mountain areas, topography, and watersheds.

Objective standards for achieving wind and rain standards

Although the final decision rests with the director, the “Operation Measures” stipulates objective weather conditions standards as a basis for judgment:

Wind power standard

According to the typhoon warning forecast of the Central Meteorological Administration, areas where the typhoon wind radius may pass through within 4 hours, the forecast is:

  • Average winds reaching level 7 or above (more than 50.4 kilometers per hour), or
  • Gusts can reach level 10 or above (above 89.4 kilometers per hour)

If any of the conditions is met, suspension of classes and classes can be considered.

Rainfall standard

For a heavy rain scenario, when the cumulative rainfall forecast for the next 24 hours reaches the following thresholds and has caused a disaster or is at risk of causing a disaster:

  • Flat ground: 350 mm
  • Mountain areas: 200 mm

⚠️ New system in 2026: Keelung City announced that starting from 2026, it will be adjusted to 200 mm regardless of mountainous areas and flat land. The reason is that the terrain of Keelung is mainly hilly, and the original standard of 350 mm for flat land no longer meets actual disaster prevention needs. It has not yet been announced whether other counties and cities will follow suit.

Timing of decision: When will it be announced?

County and city governments usually announce decisions at the following timings based on typhoon trends:

  • The afternoon to evening of the previous day (17:00–22:00): Most counties and cities will announce the typhoon that is determined to be affected the next day during this time. This is the prime time period that office workers are most concerned about
  • Early morning (05:00–06:00): If the typhoon track changes rapidly or the wind and rain suddenly intensify at night, an emergency announcement may be made before going out
  • Morning of the day (before 10:00): For typhoons that are progressive or will arrive in the afternoon, classes and classes may be suspended starting from afternoon after work or class.

The most reliable way to inquire is to directly check the official page of the “Natural Disaster Suspension of Work and Class Situation Inquiry” of the General Office of Personnel and Administration, or use this site’s “Typhoon Suspension of Work and Class Inquiry Tool” to integrate the real-time status of 22 counties and cities, saving you the trouble of searching for announcements from each county and city government one by one.

Legal significance: Is the typhoon fake a “fake”? How is the salary calculated?

The answer to this question will surprise many people: Typhoon leave is not “fake” legally. Its official name is “Public Institutions Suspension of Office or Work Due to Natural Disasters”. According to the “Key Points of Labor Attendance Management and Wage Payment in Institutions Affected by Natural Disasters” (issued by the Ministry of Labor):

Workers’ absence from work → Not counted as absenteeism or personal leave

If work is suspended during a typhoon, employers shall not treat workers’ absence as absenteeism, nor may they force workers to use personal leave, sick leave, or special leave as deductions. However, the following rules govern whether wages are paid:

SituationLabor RightsEmployer Obligations
The government announced that work is suspended and workers fail to show upIt does not count as absenteeismWages must not be paid to** (it is “not attributable to both parties”), but they are encouraged to be paid
The government announced that work is suspended and workers voluntarily attend workWork is paidWages should be increased (not less than 1 times the original salary), commonly known as “double pay”
The government has not announced a work suspension, and workers are unable to attend work due to wind and rainThey must ask for leave in advanceEmployers may deduct wages based on personal leave/special leave
Injury or financial loss during workThe case will be deemed as an occupational accidentThe employer shall be liable for occupational accident compensation

⚠️ The key point is “Whether wages must be paid” does not mean “must not be paid” - Many people mistakenly believe that no wages will be paid during typhoon leave. In fact, most companies will choose to pay wages to maintain employee relations. This is the “flexibility” provided by the law to employers rather than mandatory wage deduction regulations. If your company does not pay you, you can negotiate; if you are on duty, be sure to confirm whether your salary has been doubled.

FAQ: Frequently Asked Questions about Typhoon Holidays

Q1: When will the government announce the suspension of classes and classes? Which one is right, TV ticker or official website?

Please refer to the official inquiry page of the Personnel and Administration Office. Information from TV stations or news websites is sometimes delayed or mistakenly posted, and when it comes to disputes, announcements on the official website are the main ones.

Q2: What should I do if I go to work on the day of the typhoon holiday and my boss says “no subsidy was announced”?

According to the “Key Points on Wage Payment”, those who voluntarily work during the typhoon holiday must receive an additional salary (at least 1 time). If the employer fails to pay, the employer can appeal to the local labor bureau/labour bureau. It is recommended to keep attendance records, time clock information, and conversation records with supervisors as evidence.

Q3: How will the school make up for the typhoon holiday?

The policies of each school are different. Most elementary schools to high schools will arrange make-up classes on all day classes on Saturdays or compressed winter and summer vacations. Colleges and universities mostly handle the situation in a flexible manner, leaving it to each department to decide whether to make up classes.

Q4: What does it mean when you see “Stop going to work, stop attending classes” on the query page of the General Office of Personnel and Administration?

“Stop going to work” is targeted at workplaces of government agencies and companies; “Stop going to school” is targeted at schools at all levels. Usually syncs, but there are occasional exceptions. For example, under certain circumstances, it may be announced that “schools will be stopped but work will not be stopped.” This is often the case when there is wind and rain in the afternoon. The purpose is to allow parents time to pick up their children early.

Q5: Are injuries caused in car accidents due to collapsed road trees and flooding caused by typhoons considered occupational disasters?

If a worker is assigned by the employer to go out for work or for company business needs (such as delivery, field work), and an accident occurs on the way, he or she may claim that it is a commuting accident or an occupational accident, and the employer shall bear the liability for occupational accident compensation. It is recommended to keep the report record and medical receipts after the accident, and notify the company’s occupational accident handling window.

Conclusion: Standard actions during typhoon days

Before a typhoon approaches, it is recommended to prepare in the following order:

  1. Track the weather: Pay attention to the path forecast of the Central Meteorological Administration 2-3 days before the typhoon makes landfall.
  2. Whether the service is expected to be suspended: Determine the probability of wind and rain reaching the standard based on the passing time of the storm radius
  3. Announcement period monitoring: After 17:00 in the evening of the previous day, intensively check the page of the General Administration of Personnel or the tools of this website
  4. Confirm employer policy: whether there will be additional wages for voluntary attendance and how to deal with inability to attend work
  5. Reserve time: Make the decision to go out after the suspension of classes and classes for the day is announced, and reduce unnecessary outings during typhoons.
