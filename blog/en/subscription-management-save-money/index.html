The Hidden Subscription Monster: A 2026 Subscription Audit Checklist to Save NT$20,000 a Year

Personal Finance · March 29, 2026 · 8 min

How long has it been since you opened an app? But your monthly payment is still being deducted

2026 will be the year when subscription inflation explodes. Netflix will increase the standard plan from NT$330 to NT$380 in November 2024. The annual fee of Microsoft 365 Personal Edition will increase from NT$2,190 to NT$3,090 (a 41% increase). YouTube Premium, Spotify, and Apple Music will also increase their prices one after another. For office workers in Taiwan, “The service I ordered has increased again” has become news that they hear almost every quarter.

Surveys show that the average Taiwanese office worker pays 6-8 subscriptions a month, which often adds up to more than NT$2,000. However, more than 30% of the subscriptions are actually unused. These “sleep subscriptions” unknowingly become your fixed monthly expenses, which may cost you an extra NT$10,000-20,000 in a year. The most annoying thing is that the money is scattered among various credit card accounts and telecommunications bills, making it difficult to see the total amount at a glance.

Before you start sorting out your subscriptions, first take a look at the official monthly fees for Taiwan’s mainstream subscription services in 2026. This comparison table will be the basis for your next decision.

Step 1: Take a complete inventory of your subscriptions

Open your credit card and cell phone bills and find any recurring charges. The most easily missed thing is “telecom bill collection” - Chunghwa Telecom, Taiwan Telecom, and Far EasTone will include Netflix and Disney+ in their monthly bills, and users often forget to include them.

Organize the list according to the following categories:

  • Streaming Entertainment: Netflix, Disney+, Apple TV+, Hami Video, myVideo
  • Music: Spotify, Apple Music, KKBOX, YouTube Music
  • Cloud Storage: iCloud+, Google One, OneDrive, Dropbox
  • Productivity Tools: Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Notion Plus, Adobe Creative Cloud
  • AI Tools: ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro, Perplexity Pro, Gemini Advanced
  • Sports and Fitness: Gym monthly membership, yoga app (Down Dog, Nike Training Club Pro), sports watch subscription
  • VPN / Security: NordVPN, ExpressVPN, anti-virus software
  • Others: Switch Online, PS Plus, e-books (Kindle Unlimited, Hami Bookstore), online courses

Many people will be surprised to find that “I actually have 12 subscriptions” - 5 of them may not remember buying them at all, or they forgot to cancel after subscribing for a certain event.

Step 2: Evaluate the “actual usage” of each subscription

Ask yourself four questions for every subscription:

  1. How many times have you opened it in the past 30 days? (not “how many times do you want to use it”, but how many times have you actually opened it)
  2. If canceled, will it really affect daily life?
  3. Are there any cheaper alternatives (free version, family plan, annual subscription discount)?
  4. Can you share it with family/friends to reduce costs?

Here are a few real cases in 2026:

  • NETFLIX STANDARD PLAN NT$ 380: Worth it if you watch it ≥ 3 times a week; if you don’t watch it 5 times a month, you can switch to YouTube free content or merge it into a family account (only if you have the same address)
  • Microsoft 365 personal NT$ 3,090/year: If you only use Word + Excel, the free Google Docs/Sheets are enough; if the whole family needs Office + 1 TB cloud, change to the home version NT$ 4,190/year. Shared by 6 people, the per capita NT$ 698/year is far more cost-effective than the personal version
  • Apple Music personal NT$ 165: Family plan can be changed to NT$ 265 / 6 people = NT$ 44 per person, much lower than the personal version
  • YouTube Premium individual NT$ 199: Those who watch ads heavily + listen to videos in the background (no ads) + YouTube Music all-in-one is worth it. Light users can also block ads with Brave browser + Adblock (but mobile devices are limited)

Step 3: Execute the “Subscription Cooling-off Period”

If a subscription has not been used for more than 30 days, just cancel it and try it. Most services will retain your account and settings for 90-180 days. If you want to resubscribe in the future, you only need to click twice, but this 30-day “vacuum period” will let you see whether you really need it.

Implementation steps:

  1. Mark the next deduction date for each subscription on your mobile calendar
  2. Remind yourself 3 days before the deduction date to check whether you have actually used it this month.
  3. Cancel unused items instead of “trying for another month” (self-deception will extend to the third or fourth month)
  4. Look back 30 days after canceling and ask yourself, “Did I miss it?” If not, then cancel permanently.

Step 4: Divide the family plan between people - the real amount of money saved

Some subscriptions support family plans, but require same address verification (Spotify will start checking in 2023, Netflix will follow up in 2024). If you share it with family members or people living with you (roommates, partners), the actual monthly fee per person can be reduced to 1/3 to 1/6 of the original price:

The safest family plan participants are family members or roommates who actually live with them.

Step 5: Regular review (once every quarter)

Don’t think that “check it once and you’re done.” Three months later, you may have subscribed to a new service (seeing a limited-time offer), or you may have forgotten that a certain annual subscription is about to expire. It is recommended to set aside 15 minutes every quarter (3 months):

  • Reopen credit card bill + telecom bill
  • Compare the list to see if there are any “unrecorded deductions”
  • Annual subscription that is about to expire - decide whether to renew, set a reminder 3 days before expiration
  • Check out which services will increase in price in 2026, and evaluate whether it’s time to downgrade or cancel your subscription

Subscribe to long-term strategies in the age of inflation

2024-2026 subscription inflation tells us: “Affordable now” does not equal “affordable next year”. Each service may increase its price by 10-40% in any given November without warning. Long-term coping strategies:

  1. Single service monthly fee upper limit: Set personal standards (for example, “a single subscription shall not exceed 0.5% of monthly salary”). Exceeding the upper limit triggers review
  2. Thoughts on Subscription Annuity: Calculate the monthly fee × 12 as the annual fee. Seeing “Spotify 2,016 yuan a year” will be more realistic than “168 yuan a month”
  3. Awareness of Substitutes: If Spotify increases, try KKBOX and Apple Music; if Netflix increases, try Disney+; no subscription is “irreplaceable”
  4. Family plan priority: all services that can be divided into people are in the family plan, and only services without individual options are left in the individual plan

Added “48-hour cool-down list” before subscribing

Many subscriptions are added impulsively (seeing limited time offers, recommendations from friends, or a certain need). It is recommended to create a “48-hour cool-down list”:

  1. When you see a service you want to subscribe to, write it down first. Don’t subscribe immediately
  2. Reassess after 48 hours:
    • How many times have I thought about it in the past 48 hours?
    • Are there any existing subscriptions that already meet the same needs?
    • Is the monthly fee × 12 annual fee worth it?
    • Do I estimate it will last 3 months or 12 months?
  3. Still want to subscribe → subscribe; didn’t expect it → don’t subscribe

This 48-hour delay can block 60-70% of impulse subscriptions.

Get started: Open your credit card statement, spend 15 minutes listing all your subscriptions, and ask yourself, “Am I really using this?” The money you save in a year can be enough to buy a new phone, save for a travel fund, or get an annual health checkup.
