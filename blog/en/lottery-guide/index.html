Taiwan Lottery Complete Guide 2026: Super Lotto, Big Lotto, Daily 539 — Rules, Odds & Tax

Life Tips · January 25, 2026 · 10 min

Taiwan’s lottery turnover exceeds 100 billion yuan every year, and it is a small-dollar entertainment habit for many people. However, many lottery fans actually don’t know who the issuer is, how different the odds of winning are for different lottery types, and how much tax will be deducted when claiming the prize. This article clarifies the gameplay, lottery time, and prize collection process of the three major lottery types at once, and updates them to the latest regulations in 2026.

Taiwan Lottery: Who issues it and where are the proceeds used?

Many people mistakenly believe that “Taiwan Lottery” is issued by banks in Taiwan. This is a common mistake. The correct structure is as follows:

  • Authorized Unit: Ministry of Finance
  • Issuing institution: China Trust and Commercial Bank (CTBC Bank, taken over since 2014)
  • Operating unit: Taiwan Lottery Co., Ltd. (subsidiary of China Trust)

This three-tier structure represents: After deducting bonuses and operating expenses from lottery sales revenue, the surplus must be transferred to the “Public Welfare Lottery Surplus Distribution Fund”** and distributed to:

  • Municipality, county and city government: used for social welfare expenditures (such as subsidies for low-income households, care for the elderly, and support for people with disabilities)
  • National Pension Fund: Supplement social security financial resources
  • National Health Insurance Reserve: Reduce the financial pressure on health insurance

In other words, even if you don’t win the lottery, part of the money will go back to social welfare. Understanding this meaning will help you to view lottery consumption in a healthy way and treat it as a small amount of entertainment and public welfare participation, rather than a shortcut to escape poverty.

Although Powerball has the lowest chance of winning the jackpot (one in 22.09 million), it has the highest “overall winning rate” (approximately 11.78%, with an average of 1 winning bet for every 8.5 bets), so the expected value of small prizes is the best. Jincai 539 has no accumulation mechanism - even if you win continuously, the jackpot is still 8 million, which is suitable for players who “want to have something to look forward to every day”.

Three common lottery psychology traps:

  1. Gambler’s Fallacy: It is wrong to believe that “if 7 has not been opened recently, it will definitely be opened next period”. Each lottery drawing is an independent event, and the results of previous periods will not affect the odds of this period.
  2. Sunk Cost: “I have bought 100 issues without winning, and I should win if I buy 10 more.” This idea will make you lose more and more. The more you invest ≠ the closer you are to winning.
  3. Availability Heuristic: The media only reports on the “Lottery Lottery Shop” + the grand prize winner, making people overestimate the probability of winning. The odds of a jackpot of 1/22.09 million are 22 times lower than the odds of being struck by lightning (1/1 million), but you won’t see the “22.08 million people who didn’t win” in the news.

Rational advice: Treat lottery tickets as entertainment expenses, not as investments. Set a lottery budget every month (it is recommended that it does not exceed 10% of entertainment expenses). If you win, you will be happy and if you don’t win, you will donate it to charity (about 28% of the government surplus goes back to the social welfare fund).

Why do lottery ticket sales increase during inflation?

Observing that during the inflation period from 2022 to 2024, Taiwan Lottery sales** will increase by 8-15% annually** - this is related to the behavioral economics phenomenon of “declining relative purchasing power → psychologically more willing to gamble on a turnaround opportunity”. Taiwan Lottery’s sales exceeding NT$150 billion in 2024, a record high, is concrete evidence of this trend. But lotteries are not an anti-inflation tool and the long-term expected value is still negative. To truly combat inflation, it is more practical to return to asset allocation (ETF regular quota/high-interest living savings).

FAQ: Lottery Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: Does the lottery shop really “give out big prizes”? Which one is easier to win?

Winning the lottery is a purely random event and has no causal relationship with the location of the lottery ticket bank or the past record of winning big prizes. The media often reports that a certain lottery shop offers “new jackpots” simply because the store has a large sales volume and naturally has a higher chance of winning the jackpot. Don’t go long distances to a lottery shop just to “get the big prize”, as this will only increase your time cost.

Q2: Which one is more cost-effective among Weilicai, Big Lotto and Jincai 539?

Purely from the expected value point of view, the long-term return rate of each bet of 50 yuan for each of the three is less than 50 yuan (the difference is the government’s public welfare surplus distribution). But if we consider “entertainment vs. possibility of winning”:

  • If you want to chase the big prize and accept the extremely low probability: choose Power Color
  • If you want to have a medium chance and guarantee a bonus of more than one million: choose Big Lotto
  • If you want to have something to look forward to every day and enjoy small amounts of entertainment: choose Jincai 539

Q3: Do I have to claim it in person after winning? Can I ask a family member to pick it up on my behalf?

Single bets of less than 5,000 yuan can be collected by others with lottery tickets, no identity verification is required. Single bet exceeds 5,000 yuan Since it involves withholding income tax, the winner needs to handle it in person with his or her ID card; if entrusting someone else, the ID cards and power of attorney of both parties are required. **Major awards (above one million) are strongly recommended to be handled in person to avoid entrustment disputes.

Q4: Do I need to declare income tax again after withholding 20%?

Charitable lottery winnings are subject to separate taxation and do not need to be incorporated into the comprehensive income tax return after 20% withholding. In other words, tax is paid once it is withheld. However, it is recommended to keep the withholding voucher for inspection.

Q5: Can lottery tickets be reissued if they are lost or damaged?

Cannot be reissued. Lottery tickets are bearer securities, and losing them is equivalent to giving up the right to win. It is recommended to sign the back of the lottery ticket immediately after winning the prize and keep it in a safe place.

Conclusion: rational consumption, healthy entertainment

Before buying a lottery ticket, please remember three things:

  1. Act within your ability: Set a monthly lottery budget limit that does not exceed the proportion of entertainment expenses
  2. Understand the odds: The Powerball jackpot is 1/22.09 million, which is 22 times lower than the chance of being struck by lightning (about 1/1 million)
  3. Surplus for charity: Even if you don’t win, part of the amount will go back to social welfare and be regarded as a small amount to participate in charity.
