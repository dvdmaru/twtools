Building a 6-Month Emergency Fund on a NT$40K Salary: A Practical Guide for Taiwan Office Workers

Personal Finance · May 10, 2026 · 10 min

“If you suddenly lose your job tomorrow, how many months can your savings last?” This question is quite worrying for many office workers. According to statistics from Taiwan’s Ministry of Labor, the average unemployment cycle in 2025 will be 22.4 weeks (about 5.6 months), but less than 30% of office workers have a six-month emergency reserve fund. When something unexpected happens—losing a job, getting sick, or a family member needs money—people without a reserve have no choice but to swipe their credit cards or borrow money, and the debt snowball grows.

This article uses a standard office worker with a monthly salary of 40,000 yuan as an example to tell you how to start from scratch and save an emergency reserve of 165,000 yuan in 18 months. Information updated as of May 2026.

Why is 6 months of emergency reserve sufficient?

The most common saying on the Internet is “prepare 3-6 months of living expenses”, but in fact 3 months is obviously not enough. There are three reasons:

  • It takes an average of 5-6 months to find a job: According to statistics from the Job Bank, it takes an average of 4-7 months for office workers in Taiwan to change jobs. A three-month reserve fund cannot cover the next salary at all.
  • Unexpected expenses are bigger than expected: medical expenses, car accident compensation, emergency needs for the elderly, these are not included in the monthly fixed expenses
  • Psychological stress will ruin your job search performance: The job search anxiety when you only have enough money left to last for 1 month can easily make you accept the first offer instead of the most suitable job.

If you are a case worker, contract worker, or work of a business nature, it is recommended to wait until 9-12 months, because the income fluctuates more. If you have a mortgage or have children at home, the starting time is at least 8 months.

Calculate your emergency fund target amount

The bigger the emergency reserve, the better. The calculation formula only looks at the “minimum living expenses”, not the current consumption level. Divide your monthly expenses into three categories:

  • Necessary expenses: rent, water, electricity, gas and internet, food expenses (mainly self-catering), transportation expenses, insurance premiums
  • Semi-essential expenses: Cell phone bills, monthly gym fees, streaming subscriptions
  • Non-essential expenses: eating out, entertainment, shopping, travel

Emergency funds only count as Category 1 plus half of Category 2. Take a single office worker living in Taipei with a monthly salary of 40,000 as an example:

  • Rent 13,000 yuan, water, electricity and gas 1,500 yuan, internet 600 yuan
  • Food expenses (mainly self-cooked) 8,000 yuan, transportation expenses 1,500 yuan, insurance premium 2,000 yuan
  • Total necessary expenses are 26,600 yuan
  • Semi-necessary expenses 1,500 yuan (mobile phone 800, streaming 700)
  • Minimum monthly expenses are approximately NT$27,500, and 6-month reserve fund target is NT$165,000

For those who live at home or share a house, the amount may only be around 120,000; for those who have a mortgage or support a family, the amount will be increased. Figure out your own numbers first and your goals will make sense.

Actual distribution plan of monthly salary 40,000

The monthly salary is RMB 40,000, after deducting labor and health insurance, and the actual pension is approximately RMB 36,500. To save 165,000 yuan in 18 months, save an average of 9,200 yuan every month, which is equivalent to saving 25% of your salary. It sounds harsh, but it works in the right way:

  • Necessary expenses 26,600 yuan (73%)
  • Emergency fund savings of $9,200 (25%)
  • Entertainment flexibility NT$700 (2%)

But the “entertainment flexibility” of this plan is almost zero, which makes it unbearable. It is recommended to use the accelerated version of the “521 Rule”: changing food expenses from eating out to cooking at home can save 4,000 yuan, deactivating idle subscriptions can save 600 yuan, and bringing a kettle to work can save 5,000 yuan a year. These small adjustments add up to an additional 1,500 yuan in flexibility every month, which can last until 18 months without breaking the bank.

Where should the emergency fund be kept so that it will not be spent?

Many people fail to save money not because they earn less, but because they put their money in the wrong place - they have a salary transfer account that they use every day, but it is spent when they swipe their cards. The principle of storing emergency reserves is “you can get it, but you can’t get it smoothly”:

  • Digital account high profit margin (recommended): Taishin Richart 1.5%, Wing Fung DAWHO 1.6%, Cathay Pacific KOKO 1.5%. There is a monthly limit limit (mostly within 300,000), but the liquidity is high and the interest rate is more than 5 times better than ordinary living savings.
  • Term Savings Deposit: The 1-year annual interest rate is about 1.7%, but the interest on early termination is 20% off. Suitable for parts that are sure not to be moved within a year
  • Absolutely don’t invest in stocks, funds, or cryptocurrencies. These can fall by 30% at any time. You can just sell them at a loss when you need money urgently.

The recommended allocation is: 1 month of living expenses (27,500) can be put into a salary account for turnover, 2 months can be put into a digital account for immediate use, and the remaining 3 months can be put into a 1-year fixed deposit to earn interest.

Three Common Emergency Fund Myths

Myth 1: “I don’t need to save an emergency fund because I have a credit card limit.”

The annual interest rate of credit card revolving interest is 12-15%. If you borrow RMB 100,000, you will have to repay RMB 12,000 to RMB 15,000 in interest per year. It is equivalent to using the most expensive way to deal with emergencies, turning the problem from no money to money owed plus no money.

Myth 2: “I don’t need to save an emergency fund because I have insurance.”

Insurance claims usually take 2 weeks to 2 months, who will pay your rent during this time? Moreover, the insurance does not cover unemployment or family needs, and the coverage is limited.

Myth 3: “I can borrow it from my family”

Your family’s money is a reserve fund for you, but it is also hard-earned money for them. Borrowing money can be emotionally damaging, so avoid it if you can. The essence of an emergency fund is the sense of security that “you can get through it without relying on anyone.”

Advanced skills: Use three accounts to split management

After saving the first 165,000, it is recommended to upgrade to “three-account diversion management” so that the emergency reserve can be dynamically adjusted with changes in life:

  • Life-saving account (3 months): Put digital money into your account and only use it if you are really unemployed or hospitalized.
  • Opportunity Account (2 months): Place fixed deposits or Treasury bond ETFs, which can be used when there is a good investment opportunity
  • Buffer account (1 month): Transfer account after salary release, use it directly for temporary expenses of the month, and make up for it at the end of the month.

Do an inventory twice a year. It is recommended to use a date calculator to mark the review dates in January and July each year, confirm that the amount has kept up with the price, and re-confirm whether the proportion of the three accounts is reasonable.

FAQ

Q1: How should I save with a monthly salary of 30,000 yuan as a new employee?

Set a low threshold first, with a target of 3 months (about 90,000). Save 3,000 yuan per month and reach the target in 2 and a half years. Extend to 6 months after salary increase. The key point is to first have “the habit of saving money”. Habit is more important than the amount.

Q2: If I have a loan, should I pay it off first or save an emergency fund first?

It is recommended to proceed “at the same time”. Cards with interest rates higher than 6% will be repaid first; mortgage loans and student loans with interest rates lower than 4% can be repaid while saving an emergency fund, because you cannot rely on mortgage loans to meet your urgent needs when you are in urgent need.

Q3: Will emergency reserves be eaten up by inflation?

The long-term portion that does not move for more than 18 months can be placed in low-risk commodities such as Chunghwa Telecom special stocks and government bond ETFs.

Q4: What situation is considered “really necessary”?

Three judgment criteria: unemployment or serious illness, unexpected expenses exceeding monthly salary, and major family accidents. Buying a new mobile phone, buying a bag, or giving out red envelopes during the Chinese New Year do not count. These should come from the monthly entertainment budget.
